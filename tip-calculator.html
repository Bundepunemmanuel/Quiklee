How this works

Tip is calculated as bill × (tip percent ÷ 100). If you're splitting the table, the total (bill + tip) is then divided evenly by the number of people — see the dedicated split-bill version if you want rounded, clean per-person amounts.

18–20% is the typical range for good sit-down service in the US; 10-15% is more standard in the UK and much of Europe, where service is often already included.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I tip?

15–20% is standard for sit-down restaurant service in the US, with 20% typical for good service. In the UK and much of Europe, 10% or simply rounding up is more common, since service is often already included in the bill.

How do I calculate tip percentage?

Multiply the bill by the tip percentage, then divide by 100. An $84.50 bill with an 18% tip: 84.50 × 18 ÷ 100 = $15.21.

Who should you tip, and who shouldn't you tip?

Sit-down restaurant servers, bartenders, hairdressers, and delivery drivers are standard in the US. Tipping is generally not expected for counter-service pickup, retail staff, or in countries where service is built into the price — it varies a lot by country and industry, so when in doubt, a quick local search for the specific service is worth it.

How does a tip calculator work?

It multiplies your bill by a tip percentage to get the tip amount, adds that to the bill for a total, then — if you're splitting the table — divides that total evenly across however many people are paying.
