How to Calculate BMI Manually

The full formula, in both metric and imperial, with a worked example for each — useful if you're doing this on paper, checking homework, or just curious what the calculator above is actually doing.

Metric formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres × height in metres)

  1. Convert height from centimetres to metres by dividing by 100. Example: 175cm ÷ 100 = 1.75m.
  2. Square the height in metres. Example: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625.
  3. Divide weight in kilograms by that number. Example: 70kg ÷ 3.0625 = 22.86.
  4. That result, 22.86, is the BMI.

Imperial formula

BMI = (weight in pounds ÷ (height in inches × height in inches)) × 703

  1. Convert height fully to inches. Example: 5'9" = 69 inches.
  2. Square the height in inches. Example: 69 × 69 = 4,761.
  3. Divide weight in pounds by that number, then multiply by 703. Example: (154 ÷ 4,761) × 703 ≈ 22.74.
  4. That result is the BMI — the 703 constant exists purely to make the imperial formula land on the same scale as the metric one.

The 703 multiplier trips people up most often — it's not arbitrary, it's the unit conversion factor (1kg/m² = 703 lb/in²) baked into the formula so you don't have to convert to metric first.
